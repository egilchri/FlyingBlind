The matchbox scanner

What changed

  • Power bank → built-in battery. A flat 800 mAh rechargeable battery (40 × 30 × 6 mm) sits behind the sensor. It charges through a USB-C port on the side.
  • ESP32 DevKit → XIAO ESP32-S3, a thumbnail-size ESP32 (21 × 17.5 mm) that also charges the battery.
  • Buzzer module → bare 12 mm buzzer under a grille on top.
  • A tiny 5 V booster board, because the battery gives 3.7–4.2 V and the sensor wants about 5 V.
  • An on/off slide switch, a lanyard loop, and a raised arrow on top pointing the way the sensor faces.

What it costs

  • Soldering. About a dozen joints: battery, switch, booster, buzzer and sensor wires. It's no longer a plug-together build.
  • A case. 3D-printed (a library makerspace or an online print service), or a small project box cut to fit.
  • A small firmware change. The XIAO uses different pin numbers. A low-battery warning beep would also be worth adding.
  • Battery care. Use a battery with a built-in protection circuit. Battery life and weight here are estimates.
