Made to be used by touch

  • Direction comes from the grip. The beam leaves the front along the line of the handle, so it points where the arm points, like a flashlight.
  • Front and back feel different. A wide round head at the front, a narrow handle behind it, and a lanyard loop at the tail.
  • A click button under the thumb, on top of the head with a raised rim around it, switches it on and off. A short chirp at switch-on confirms it's working.
  • The buzzer grille faces up on top of the head, in front of the hand, so the hand doesn't muffle the tone.
  • No loose wires outside the case, so nothing snags. If the sensor stops working, the firmware plays a low tick every few seconds, so a fault isn't mistaken for "nothing there".

Parts and trade-offs

  • Same as the wand: TF-Luna sensor, buzzer module, jumper wires and a lipstick power bank.
  • XIAO ESP32-S3 with its pins already soldered on, in place of the larger ESP32 board, so the head stays small. The same firmware runs on it; it picks the XIAO's pins by itself.
  • A short USB cable with an inline on/off switch joins the power bank to the board. Its button pokes through the case under the thumb.
  • A 3D-printed case in three parts: body, front bezel and tail cap, with no supports needed. The tail cap comes off to slide the power bank out for charging. Print files.
  • This is a 3D model at true size, not a photo or a finished design. Weight and battery life are estimates.
