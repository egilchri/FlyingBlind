The pocket scanner

A phone power bank becomes the handle. The sensor sits at the front, the little computer and the buzzer ride on top, and everything is held on with foam tape. It rests in your palm like a phone. Point it and listen.

What you're looking at

About 12 × 6.5 × 4 cm · 200–250 g, mostly the power bank

  • LiDAR sensor at the front, facing forward, with its two lenses. It measures 100 times a second.
  • ESP32 board, mounted upside down so the jumper wires push straight onto its pins. No soldering.
  • Buzzer (the black cylinder on the blue board). It plays the tone: closer is higher.
  • Power bank as the handle. A short USB cable powers the board, and the bank's button switches it all on.
  • The red cone shows where it's pointing. The real beam is invisible infrared.

Still to settle

  • This is a 3D model at true size, not a photo. The hand is an adult's, shown for scale. The real sensor's wire colors and the board's color depend on the seller.
  • The buzzer firmware is written (buzzer on pin 25), but it hasn't been run on a real board yet.
  • Double-sided foam tape isn't on the parts list yet. Any roll will do, about $5.
